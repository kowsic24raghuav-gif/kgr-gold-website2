From dorééé to bullion

dorééé arrives

The gold we receive for purification is called a dorééé bar: an alloy of gold and other metals. It is extracted from mined ore and is not yet pure, so impurities have to be removed.

Refined in fire

The refining process removes those impurities. Our refinery accepts high, medium and low grade inputs, from mined dorééé to jewellery scrap, and turns them into gold in its purest form.

Cast into bullion

Refined gold is cast into bars and ingots. Bullion is gold officially recognised as at least 99.5% or 99.9% pure, and it comes in a variety of denominations.

Ready to hold

Finished bars leave in .999 or 916 purity. Bullion is the classic hedge in an inflationary economy: solid value that keeps pace with prices, and very liquid.

The market we serve

Gold = asset + money

Gold doesn't just signify jewellery value. It has long been used as a medium of exchange, or money. It can be beautifully shaped and sculpted, it conducts electricity and it does not tarnish, which makes it the metal of choice for the industrial, medical and technology industries. Almost every established culture has used gold to symbolise power, beauty, purity and accomplishment.

Silver bullion

Silver bullion is highly concentrated silver, typically defined as 99.9% pure. It comes as bars, coins or "rounds", and it derives its value solely from its metal content, with no artistic or collectible value. One troy ounce is the most popular size, but weights range from one gram to 10 kilograms.
